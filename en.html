How many people live on Earth?

More than 8.3 billion (UN World Population Prospects 2024). About four babies are born every second and about two people die; the counter on the globe runs live. Half of all people live inside one circle over South and East Asia that covers only about 7% of the Earth’s surface. Where the lights sit comes from the 1 km population grid of the Global Human Settlement Layer (European Commission, JRC).

How many animals are there?

More than half a sextillion, almost all of them tiny: nematodes in the soil, copepods in the sea, insects everywhere. For every person there are about 2.4 million ants and six wild birds. By weight, only about 4% of all mammals are wild; the rest are farm animals and people (Bar-On et al. 2018). Every group shows how sure its number is: counted, study estimate or rough estimate, with a range where science is unsure.
